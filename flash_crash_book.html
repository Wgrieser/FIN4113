The order book

Each bar is a resting bid. Selling eats them from the top down. Real buyers trickle back slowly. When high-frequency firms pull their quotes, the bars vanish without anyone having bought anything.

What you just watched

Volume is not depth. Compare the two numbers above. On the real May 6, high-frequency firms traded more than 27,000 contracts in fourteen seconds and bought about 200 of them net. Everything else was the same contracts changing hands between machines.
The algorithm was reading the wrong number. It was told to sell 9 percent of the previous minute's volume, with no price limit and no time limit. Churn looks exactly like demand if volume is all you measure, so the faster the machines passed contracts around, the faster the fund sold into a market with nobody left in it.
Switch on the price bands and run it again. Nothing about fear changes. The band simply stops a price from printing far away from the market and pauses trading instead, which is why in August 2024 the VIX went higher than it did in 2010 and no large ETF detached from the index.

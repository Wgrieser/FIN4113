The ten short sellers

All ten shorted at $20. They differ only in how much cash they have behind the position, which fixes the price at which their broker forces them out. Watch the price climb past those numbers one by one.

Try these three in order

1. News at +20%, short interest at 140%. The news alone takes the price from $20 to $24, which is past exactly one fund's breaking point. That one fund buys, and the buying does everything after that. The price ends near $350 off a 20 percent piece of news.
2. Leave the news at +20%, drag short interest down to 30%. Identical news. Identical breaking points. Three funds go and it stops near $29, because each one buying 0.3 million shares barely moves anything. The cascade needs fuel and there is none.
3. Now crank the news to +50% and leave short interest at 30%. Much bigger shock, and it still dies. The news gets you a few more funds and then the same stall. That is the point: the shock only has to break the first domino. After that the shock is irrelevant and the forced buying is the whole story.
So which number matters? Not the news. A short seller who is wrong has to buy, and how much they have to buy is set by how much was sold short in the first place. That is why short interest above the float is the number people watched in January 2021, and not the headline that started it.

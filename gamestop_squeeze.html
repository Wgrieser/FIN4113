The ten short sellers

Each one borrowed a share and sold it, betting the price would fall. Each has a cushion of cash with their broker. When the price rises past that cushion, the broker does not ask politely.

What you just watched

A short seller's demand curve points the wrong way. To close a short you have to buy. So a rising price forces the people betting against the stock to become buyers, which raises the price, which forces the next one. Everyone else can wait. They cannot.
Push the slider past 100 percent and it stops being a market. More shares have been sold short than exist to buy, because a borrowed share can be lent again. The shorts collectively owe more stock than is available at any price, so the only thing that can clear the imbalance is the price itself.
Now compare it to our own game. In the Activist Hedge Fund Game nobody could short at all, and prices sat above the six dollar fundamental for six straight rounds. Here people could short, heavily, and the price still left fundamentals behind. Either the pessimists cannot act, or they can act and then get forced out. Both roads end with an optimist setting the price.

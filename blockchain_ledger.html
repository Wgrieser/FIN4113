Tamper With the Ledger

You have never held money. You have only ever held somebody's promise to update a record. Here is the record.

Step 1. Who keeps the ledger?

Your bank knows your balance.

You do not. You hold a promise to update a record that somebody else keeps. Same for DTCC and the shares you own, and for the clearinghouse and your futures position. One keeper, one copy, total trust.

Cash cannot be spent twice.

Handing over a bill removes it from your pocket. A file copies perfectly and forever, so digital money cannot work on possession. It has to work on a list of who has what. That list is the whole problem.

Step 2. Now try to rewrite history

A blockchain gives the list to everyone and chains each entry to the one before it. Edit any block below and watch what happens to the blocks after it.

Each fingerprint is computed from the block's own text plus the fingerprint before it. Change one character and every fingerprint downstream stops matching. To make a lie stick you would have to redo all of them, faster than thousands of other computers are extending the real chain. That is the entire security model.

Step 3. The part nobody puts on the poster

Here is a block. It is correctly formed, correctly signed and correctly chained. Press verify.

Block #9  ·  "FTX holds 100% of customer deposits. Nothing has been lent to Alameda."
 
The chain was telling the truth. It said nobody had altered the record. It never said the record was correct. Nothing a network can check tells you whether the money was actually there, because the transfer to Alameda was never written down here. The blockchain worked perfectly for the entire duration of the fraud.

What to take away

Blockchain changes who keeps the ledger. It changes nothing about what can go wrong with the things the ledger describes. Verifying a record is not the same as verifying a fact, which is the Market for Lemons problem sitting exactly where it always sat.
It buys one real thing. The asset and the payment move in the same instant, so neither side delivers and waits. That is the risk that killed Bank Herstatt in 1974, and it is the same job a futures clearinghouse does by standing in the middle instead.
And it costs the middleman's other jobs. Nobody to reverse a mistake, nobody to lend across the settlement gap, no lender of last resort. Which is why crypto rebuilt exchanges, and why one of them was holding eight billion dollars of customer money.
